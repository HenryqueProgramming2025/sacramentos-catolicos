Baptism: Grace and Water.

"Truly, truly, I say to you, unless one is born of water and the Spirit, he cannot enter the kingdom of God."

John 3:5


What is Baptism?

Baptism is the first sacrament and the gateway to the Christian life. Through this efficacious sign of grace, we die to ourselves and we start living to God, as His adopted sons and daughters. We are no longer creatures, we are what we were always meant to be: children of God.

The Biblical Foundation

Baptism is rooted in Sacred Scripture. Not once, not twice, but several times. You, my Catholic friend, saw one of the verses in the header: "Truly, truly, I say to you, unless one is born of water and the Spirit, he cannot enter the Kingdom of God" (John 3:5).

Jesus Christ, Our Lord and Savior, before ascending to Heaven, commanded His apostles: "Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit" (Matthew 28:19).

This verse explains the profound mystery of what happens in Baptism: "Do you not know that all of us who have been baptized into Christ Jesus were baptized into his death? We were buried therefore with him by baptism into death, so that as Christ was raised from the dead by the glory of the Father, we too might walk in newness of life" (Romans 6:3-4).

By being baptized, we receive the third person of the Trinity, the Holy Spirit: "And Peter said to them, 'Repent, and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of your sins; and you shall receive the gift of the Holy Spirit'" (Acts 2:38).

Can Children be Baptized?

One of the disagreements between Protestants and Catholics is the Baptism of children. Although they accept this sacrament as valid, the majority of them do not see infant baptism as necessary, because the little ones have no understanding of it. They also claim it is not rooted in Sacred Scripture, which is the only authority Protestants follow, rejecting Oral Tradition and the Magisterium.

We will not see the word "infant" next to the word "baptism" in the Bible, but that does not mean the concept is not there, as Sacred Scripture has its profound nuances. When we read the Scriptures, we can see verses showing that entire families were baptized when the head of the house believed (Acts 16:15, Acts 16:33, 1 Corinthians 1:16).

Jesus, our Lord and Savior, welcomed children, saying the Kingdom of God belongs to them (Mark 10:14). The Church does not exclude infants from entry into that Kingdom.
