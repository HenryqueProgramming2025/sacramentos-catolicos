Confirmation: Strengthening the Faith

"And there appeared to them tongues as of fire, distributed and resting on each one of them. And they were all filled with the Holy Spirit."

- Acts 2:3-4


What is Confirmation?

Confirmation is the second sacrament of Christian initiation. It is the moment when the Holy Spirit, third person of the Trinity, descends upon the believer with power, perfecting the grace received at Baptism. If Baptism makes us children of God, Confirmation encourages us to defend it with the strength of a soldier of Christ.

The Biblical Foundation

This sacrament is not an invention of the Catholic Church; it comes directly from the Sacred Scripture. In the Acts of the Apostles, we can see them laying their hands on people who had already been baptized so they could receive the Holy Spirit (Acts 8:17). Jesus also promised this power to His followers (Acts 1:8).

The Effects of Confirmation

When a person is confirmed, they receive the fullness of the Holy Spirit, including the seven gifts (Wisdom, Understanding, Counsel, Fortitude, Knowledge, Piety and Fear of the Lord). The soul is strengthened to live the faith with maturity and courage. When baptized, we are children. When confirmed, we are mature soldiers.

Conclusion

Confirmation is not the end of the road. Through Baptism, the first of the Sacraments, we are more intimately connected with our Heavenly Father. Through Confirmation, we receive the fullness of the Holy Spirit and maturity to defend the faith handed down for two millennia.
